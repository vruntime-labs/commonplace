# Code of Conduct

## Our commitment

Commonplace is a community project built around shared knowledge. We want participation to be welcoming, constructive, and respectful for people with different backgrounds, experience levels, identities, and perspectives.

We are committed to providing a positive environment where beginners can ask questions, experienced contributors can share expertise, and disagreements can be handled thoughtfully.

## Expected behaviour

Examples of behaviour that help create a healthy community include:

- Being respectful, considerate, and constructive.
- Welcoming questions and beginner contributions.
- Critiquing ideas and work without attacking people.
- Explaining technical disagreements with evidence and context.
- Giving credit to authors and respecting intellectual property.
- Accepting feedback and responding professionally.
- Helping keep discussions focused, accessible, and useful.

## Unacceptable behaviour

The following behaviour is not acceptable:

- Harassment, discrimination, or hateful conduct.
- Personal attacks, insults, intimidation, or threats.
- Sexual harassment or unwelcome sexual attention.
- Publishing or threatening to publish another person's private information without consent.
- Deliberate disruption, repeated bad-faith behaviour, or targeted abuse.
- Sharing malicious content or encouraging harm against contributors or users.
- Retaliation against someone who raises a concern in good faith.
- Ignoring reasonable moderation instructions.

## Scope

This Code of Conduct applies to project spaces, including:

- The Commonplace GitHub repository, issues, and pull requests.
- Future project discussions and community spaces.
- Official project communication channels.
- Situations where someone is representing Commonplace or its community.

## Reporting

If you experience or witness behaviour that violates this Code, contact the project maintainers through a private GitHub communication channel.

Please do not post sensitive personal information or private evidence in a public issue. When reporting, include relevant context and links where it is safe to do so.

Reports will be handled as confidentially as practical. The project will not promise absolute confidentiality where disclosure is necessary to investigate or address a serious concern.

## Enforcement

Maintainers are responsible for reviewing reports and deciding on appropriate action. Depending on the circumstances, action may include:

1. A request to stop the behaviour or a private warning.
2. Removal or editing of harmful content.
3. Temporary restrictions from project spaces.
4. Permanent removal from project spaces for serious or repeated violations.

Maintainers should consider the nature, severity, context, and impact of the behaviour. People involved should be treated fairly and respectfully.
